# Evidence appraisal and intervention mapping

Evidence checked: 21 October 2026. This document supports an *educational sound-enrichment prototype*. It is not a clinical guideline or an evidence synthesis equivalent to a systematic review.

| Feature or statement | Evidence and interpretation | Design decision |
|---|---|---|
| Therapeutic use of sound for self-care | VA/DoD 2024 recommendation 12 is **weak for**. It describes possible reductions in the self-perceived impact of tinnitus. The evidence was assessed as low quality and benefits are variable. | Offer optional, comfortable background sound. Avoid cure claims. |
| Sound enrichment and audiology education | VA/DoD 2024 recommendation 17 is **weak for** sound enrichment with ongoing directed tinnitus education by an audiologist. | Encourage professional assessment. In-app educational information is not a substitute for audiologist-delivered counselling. |
| Stand-alone sound therapy | NICE NG155 (2020) could not make practice recommendations for sound therapy, given insufficient clinical and cost-effectiveness evidence. Sereda et al. (2018) found low-certainty evidence, with insufficient basis for judging benefit over usual/no device or differences between devices. | No claim that white, pink, brown or filtered sound outperforms alternatives. |
| Notched or spectrally altered music | VA/DoD 2024 recommendation 13 found insufficient evidence to recommend for or against. | Not implemented. |
| Tinnitus pitch matching | NICE NG155 recommendation 1.4.5 advises against psychoacoustic pitch/loudness matching for routine tinnitus assessment. | No tinnitus-pitch matching feature. Tone control is an optional low-level texture only. |
| CBT for impact/distress | Fuller et al. (2020), 28 trials and 2,733 participants, found low- to moderate-certainty evidence for reducing tinnitus-related quality-of-life impact at end of treatment. | Provide a prompt to consider CBT and other clinically appropriate support. |
| Hearing aids when hearing loss is present | NICE NG155 recommends offering amplification where hearing loss impairs communication and consideration in some other cases with hearing loss. | Encourage formal audiological assessment, not unguided amplification. |
| Digital peak control | **Engineering precaution only**, not a clinically derived exposure threshold. | Audio peaks are capped numerically, but the app makes no SPL safety claim. |
| Short sessions and diary | No validated duration or change-score threshold adopted from these guidelines. | Timer is user selected, 5–60 min; diary is descriptive and unvalidated. |

## Clinical and engineering cautions

The generated waveform is uncalibrated. A nominal peak of 5% corresponds to approximately −26 dBFS peak in the WAV file, but **not to a measured dB SPL at the ear**. Hearing devices, headphone impedances, output amplifiers and user volume settings vary. A low digital signal cannot by itself guarantee hearing safety. Start with device volume at minimum and avoid efforts to mask tinnitus completely. Stop if discomfort, hyperacusis or symptom worsening occurs.

The tones do not reproduce evidence-based acoustic neuromodulation protocols. Differences between the downloaded WAV generator and the Web Audio generator are intentional implementation choices and are not standardised or clinically evaluated.
